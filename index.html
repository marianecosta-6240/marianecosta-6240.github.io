How I Work

How I run a launch

Every launch I run follows the same backbone, sized up or down depending on the client.

Warm-up, before a single ad runs

I map the objections the audience already has and build content that answers them without sounding like a pitch. This is where a "seed" launch and a "classic" launch split — a seed launch grows an audience slowly through weeks of free content; a classic launch compresses the same trust-building into one tight week of live events.

The schedule

Every email, WhatsApp message, and ad has a day, a time, and a reason tied to what happened right before it — someone who watched the webinar gets a different message than someone who registered and never showed up. I build this before the launch opens, because once it's open there's no time left to think.

Traffic runs in parallel, not after

I watch cost-per-lead and page conversion daily during the capture window, and I'd rather kill an underperforming ad on day two than defend a bad number on day ten.

The close

The last 48 hours of a cart window decide more of the result than the first week does. Urgency has to feel earned there, not manufactured — and that only works if everything before it was honest.

Building With AI

I don't just use AI — I build with it

Three systems I've built and run day to day:

A personal AI copilot

I built and use daily a knowledge-grounded AI copilot trained on the frameworks I work in — coaching methodology, neuroscience, personality science — so research and first drafts take minutes, not days.

A launch orchestrator

A dedicated agent manages the operational side of every launch: it loads the right process for each phase, keeps the notification schedule on track, and flags what needs a human decision instead of waiting to be asked.

A production system for content and ads

A library of task-specific AI agents handles first-pass carousels, ad creative, and campaign structuring — built so output stays on-brand and on-voice without a human doing every repetitive step by hand.

This is why I call PsicoAds AI-native: not tools bolted onto a process, but a process I rebuilt around what AI actually does well — with me deciding what it shouldn't.

Selected Work

Launches I've run

Client names are withheld by agreement — the work and the results are real.

A medical-education client

Specialist physicians teaching other doctors

The challenge. The audience was made of doctors — clinically trained to distrust anything that reads as a sales pitch, with little patience for a funnel built for a general audience.

What I did. I restructured the launch around education first: content that taught before it sold, copy checked line by line so nothing could be mistaken for a medical claim, and a notification schedule timed around when doctors actually check their phones between patients.

The result. Multiple launches run on this model, with paid traffic scaled up over time while lead cost held steady — proof the audience was never the obstacle, the approach was.

A corporate leadership client

B2B performance and HR audience, LinkedIn-first

The challenge. A LinkedIn-native audience with a long consideration cycle — nobody buys a leadership program off the strength of one ad.

What I did. I built a content cadence that did the convincing before the launch ever opened, then ran a warm-lead capture sequence that didn't lean on artificial urgency to close.

The result. Consistent lead capture into every launch window, from an audience that's normally the hardest to move fast.

My own education brand

Personal development and neurodivergence education

The challenge. This is the brand I run myself, which means there's no client to point to when a launch underperforms.

What I did. Every notification, every ad, every page — planned and shipped by me, launch after launch, in a niche where trust is the entire product.

The result. It's the clearest proof I have that the process holds up end to end, because there was nowhere to hide if it didn't.
